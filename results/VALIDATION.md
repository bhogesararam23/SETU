# SETU - validation report

Generated by `python -m setu run`. Site **Rajkot, Gujarat**, 40 nodes, 1772 hours of background, action level 15 ug/m3 PM2.5 (WHO 2021 24-hour guideline). For reference: the NAAQS 24-hour limit is 60 ug/m3 and the WHO 2021 24-hour guideline is 15 ug/m3.

## What this run can and cannot support

| Claim | Supported? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The calibration maths recovers a known signal | **Yes** | Ground truth is known by construction in the twin |
| The attribution separates sources by size mode | **Yes, partially** | 79.0% accuracy on 22,720 node-hours against 0.20 chance |
| The absolute source shares match receptor studies | **No** | See the shares table; the twin's injection rates are not tuned to a published apportionment, so shares measure the twin, not the city |
| The error figures are instrument-compliance results | **No** | The reference is CAMS reanalysis at ~10 km, not a beta-gauge station. CPCB was unreachable from the development network |
| Triggering beats a fixed roster at equal cost | **Conditionally** | Only when the lever is short-lived and the source is episodic; both null results are shown below |

## 2. Calibration transfer (nodes that never saw a reference)

38 nodes calibrated by pair-based transfer from a co-located donor. Mean overlap R^2 0.971, mean MAE 9.89 -> 1.90 ug/m3.

This is what lets coverage scale without a reference station on every pole - the bottleneck the whole project exists to get around.

The claim worth attacking is not that transfer works, but that it works *away from the donor*. Scored by donor distance:

| Donor distance | Nodes | Mean distance | MAE raw | MAE transferred | Overlap R^2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| within 1.5 pitches | 2 | 320 m | 5.85 | 1.13 | 0.983 |
| 1.5-2.5 pitches | 4 | 678 m | 7.38 | 1.08 | 0.984 |
| 2.5-3.5 pitches | 5 | 1012 m | 7.23 | 1.99 | 0.983 |
| over 3.5 pitches | 27 | 1787 m | 11.05 | 2.06 | 0.965 |

Pearson r between donor distance and post-transfer MAE: **+0.263** across 38 nodes, out to 2499 m. A large positive value would mean the transfer is really a co-location effect wearing a longer hat; this is the number that would show it.

### Share cross-check against receptor studies - FAILS for two sources

| Source | SETU share | Published range | Within? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| road_dust | 0.595 | 0.02-0.45 | **no** |
| construction | 0.041 | 0.00-0.20 | yes |
| open_burning | 0.011 | 0.00-0.30 | yes |
| secondary | 0.325 | 0.10-0.40 | yes |
| traffic_combustion | 0.028 | 0.05-0.25 | **no** |

Road dust is over-represented and traffic combustion under-represented. The cause is twin fidelity rather than discrimination: the twin injects a road-dust increment at any node whose seeded road factor exceeds 1.0, so road_dust is simply the most frequent labelled source in the synthetic record. Within-class discrimination is measured above and holds. Absolute shares cannot be validated until the injection rates are tuned to a published apportionment - tracked in docs/LIMITATIONS.md.

## 6. Does triggering beat a roster, at equal cost?

Both arms get the same number of passes per day. The roster spaces them from a 05:00 shift start; the trigger places them on the hours the grid actually breaches.

Basis: hours where the coarse fraction cleared 30 ug/m3 and the attributed source was road dust or construction - 17 distinct hours in this window. The engine above was driven at 15 ug/m3.

| Lever (effective window) | Roster coverage | Triggered | Gain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Water sprinkling, 62 min | 0.12 | 0.24 | +0.12 |
| Vacuum sweeping, 150 min | 0.18 | 0.35 | +0.18 |
| CaCl2 suppressant, ~20 h | 1.00 | 1.00 | +0.00 |

The suppressant row is the honest one. A lever that lasts a day does not care where you put it, so triggering earns nothing there.

Scored instead on continuously road-proximate dust, present across 24 hours a day, the gain is +0.00. That is the unfavourable case and it is stated rather than omitted: where a source runs around the clock, no placement strategy helps and the only lever that does is a longer-lived one. Triggering pays off on the episodic sources - construction campaigns and burning nights - and not on the chronic one.

## 7. Drift and quarantine

40 of 40 nodes raised a CUSUM alarm against their own calibration residual.

Quarantined: rajkot-00, rajkot-01, rajkot-02, rajkot-03, rajkot-05, rajkot-06, rajkot-07, rajkot-08, rajkot-10, rajkot-11, rajkot-12, rajkot-13, rajkot-15, rajkot-16, rajkot-17, rajkot-18, rajkot-20, rajkot-21, rajkot-22, rajkot-23, rajkot-25, rajkot-26, rajkot-27, rajkot-28, rajkot-30, rajkot-31, rajkot-32, rajkot-33, rajkot-35, rajkot-36, rajkot-37, rajkot-38, rajkot-40, rajkot-41, rajkot-42, rajkot-43, rajkot-45, rajkot-46, rajkot-47, rajkot-48


**This is a finding, not a passing grade.** More than half the grid tripped its drift monitor within the evaluation window. The calibration was fitted on the monsoon and scored on the post-monsoon period, and the residual relationship moved with it - so a four-month-old calibration does not survive a season change. Operationally that means recalibration is periodic, not one-off. The cost stays low because only the co-location step needs a vehicle: one reference visit refreshes every node transferred from it.

Drift is the documented reason CPCB does not accept low-cost data for regulatory use, so a monitor that cannot notice its own decay is not deployable regardless of its initial accuracy.
