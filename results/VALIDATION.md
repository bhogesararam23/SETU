# SETU - validation report

Generated by `python -m setu run`. Site **Rajkot, Gujarat**, 40 nodes, 1772 hours of background, action level 15 ug/m3 PM2.5 (WHO 2021 24-hour guideline). For reference: the NAAQS 24-hour limit is 60 ug/m3 and the WHO 2021 24-hour guideline is 15 ug/m3.

## What this run can and cannot support

| Claim | Supported? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The calibration maths recovers a known signal | **Yes** | Ground truth is known by construction in the twin |
| The attribution separates sources by size mode | **Yes, partially** | 60.0% accuracy on 15,451 node-hours against 0.20 chance |
| The absolute source shares match receptor studies | **No, fails for secondary** | The twin's injection rates were tuned to a published apportionment, so the shares measure the tuned twin, not the city - see the shares table and docs/LIMITATIONS.md |
| The error figures are instrument-compliance results | **No** | The reference is CAMS reanalysis at ~10 km, not a beta-gauge station. CPCB was unreachable from the development network |
| Triggering beats a fixed roster at equal cost | **Conditionally** | Only when the lever is short-lived and the source is episodic; both null results are shown below |
| The forecast beats persistence | **No, pooled** | Pooled skill -0.092 to -0.016 at 1/3/6 h, but the fold nearest its training data scores +0.167 to +0.300. Read as a claim about a refitted deployment, not a fixed model - section 4 has the decay curve |

Skill falls as the score slice moves away from the training data, which is the finding: the forecast is only as good as the freshness of its fit. A model fitted once at the start of the window and never refitted loses to persistence outright on this data at every horizon - the run-wide negative-skill result that made walk-forward necessary. Operationally that is the same fact the drift monitor reports about calibration: both decay with the season, and both are refreshed by the same periodic refit.

The band is split conformal at nominal 80% coverage: the point prediction plus or minus the held-out quantile of absolute residuals. Measured coverage runs 0.658 to 0.729 across horizons, and falls short of it at every horizon. Coverage is marginal over the calibration slice, not conditional on any single hour, and the width column is what that coverage costs in decisiveness - a band wide enough to straddle the action threshold at all times is honest and useless, and the response engine's uncertainty gate is what refuses to act on it.

Short coverage is the consequence of scoring out of sample, not a broken band. Each fold's band is calibrated on the slice before its score slice, so a regime shift absorbed by the calibration slice widens the band but a shift after it does not; the residual distribution moves and coverage drops with it. A band that quotes nominal coverage here would be the in-sample figure, which is exactly the number this section stopped trusting.

Pooled skill is at or below zero at every horizon, and that is the headline number: across the whole window this model does not beat persistence. The fold table above is why. Only the fold trained nearest to its own score slice is positive; the two folds scored further from their training data are not, and they outnumber it two to one. Read pooled, that averages into a wash. Read per fold, it says the deployed system is worth using exactly as long as it is refitted on current data, which the pipeline does and the pilot's weekly cadence would too.

Chen et al., Sci. Rep. 14 (2024) report RMSE 19.11 at 7h on dense reference networks; these figures are 40 low-cost nodes on a 320 m grid and are not comparable to them.

Nodes where the model beats persistence outright: 3 of 40 at 1h, 2 of 40 at 3h, 2 of 40 at 6h. The pooled skill above is a mean over nodes and hours, and a mean of zero is ambiguous between a grid that is uniformly marginal and one that is split between winners and losers; this is the count that tells them apart, and the bias column is the sign check on the same rows.

Diagnostics: 40 of 40 nodes scored across 120 walk-forward fits, 0 nodes and 0 folds skipped as too thin. 960 node-hours were lost to the 24-hour lag warm-up and 0 had no usable meteorology at all; 0 short weather gaps were bridged across up to three hours, and longer ones were left missing.

## 6. Does triggering beat a roster, at equal cost?

Both arms get the same number of passes per day. The roster spaces them from a 05:00 shift start; the trigger places them on the hours the grid actually breaches.

Basis: hours where the coarse fraction cleared 30 ug/m3 and the attributed source was road dust or construction - 16 distinct hours in this window. The engine above was driven at 15 ug/m3.

| Lever (effective window) | Roster coverage | Triggered | Gain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Water sprinkling, 62 min | 0.12 | 0.25 | +0.12 |
| Vacuum sweeping, 150 min | 0.19 | 0.38 | +0.19 |
| CaCl2 suppressant, ~20 h | 1.00 | 1.00 | +0.00 |

The suppressant row is the honest one. A lever that lasts a day does not care where you put it, so triggering earns nothing there.

Scored instead on continuously road-proximate dust, present across 22 hours a day, the gain is +0.00. That is the unfavourable case and it is stated rather than omitted: where a source runs around the clock, no placement strategy helps and the only lever that does is a longer-lived one. Triggering pays off on the episodic sources - construction campaigns and burning nights - and not on the chronic one.

**This is a finding, not a passing grade.** More than half the grid tripped its drift monitor within the evaluation window. The calibration was fitted on the monsoon and scored on the post-monsoon period, and the residual relationship moved with it - so a four-month-old calibration does not survive a season change. Operationally that means recalibration is periodic, not one-off. The cost stays low because only the co-location step needs a vehicle: one reference visit refreshes every node transferred from it.

Drift is the documented reason CPCB does not accept low-cost data for regulatory use, so a monitor that cannot notice its own decay is not deployable regardless of its initial accuracy.
